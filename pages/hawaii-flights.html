        Why People Fly to Hawaii

        Hawaii is a chain of volcanic islands in the middle of the Pacific, about five and a half hours in the air from the West Coast. Six of them take visitors, and they are not interchangeable. Oahu has the city, the history and most of the flights. Maui has the resorts and the road to Hana. Kauai has the cliffs, and the Big Island has active lava and observatories on Mauna Kea. Between them you get black sand, rainforest, high desert and working cattle country. Hawaiian language, hula and food are practiced here rather than performed, and the tourism numbers keep rising.

        Getting to Hawaii

        • By Air

          Most flights land at Daniel K. Inouye International Airport (HNL) in Honolulu. Maui is served by Kahului (OGG), the Big Island by Kona (KOA) and Hilo (ITO), and Kauai by Lihue (LIH). Several of those take direct flights from the mainland, so you do not always have to route through Honolulu.

        • By Cruise

          Ships run inter-island loops calling at Oahu, Maui, the Big Island and Kauai. It is one way to see four islands without packing and unpacking four times.

        • By Road

          There is no road to Hawaii and no ferry between most of the islands, so island hops are flights. Once you land, a rental car is how you get around; on Maui and Kauai the coast road is half the reason to be there.

        Hawaii's Airports

        Every inhabited island has an airport, and the hops between them take about half an hour in the air. Honolulu handles the long-haul traffic and most of the connections. Kahului, Kona and Lihue also take direct mainland flights. The rest are small fields built for short island runs. Here is what you are choosing between:

        • Daniel K. Inouye International Airport (HNL)

        • Hilo International Airport (ITO)

        • Kahului Airport (OGG)

        • Ellison Onizuka Kona International Airport (KOA)

        • Lihue Airport (LIH)

        • Molokai Airport (MKK)

        • Lanai Airport (LNY)

        • Waimea-Kohala Airport (MUE)

        • Kalaupapa Airport (LUP)

        • Hana Airport (HNM)
