Mexican Fiesta

8 Nights 9 Days

Overview

Mexico holds ancient ruins, long beaches, big cities and small colonial towns, with mariachi bands playing in the squares and tortillas cooked to order. This tour starts in Mexico City, the capital, where a local representative meets you and takes you to the hotel. Once you have checked in, the rest of that day is yours, and the historic centre is a short walk away. Dinner is included on the first night. The trip opens out from there, moving south and east to the Yucatan and finishing on the Caribbean coast.

Itinerary

Day 1

Arrive in Mexico City.

Mexico is ruins, long beaches, big cities and colonial towns, with mariachi bands in the squares and tortillas pressed to order. It is a country most visitors feel they already half know.

You land in Mexico City, the capital. Our local representative meets you and takes you to the hotel to check in. After that the day is yours, and the historic centre is close enough to walk.

Overnight in Mexico City. (D)

Note: check-in is from 1500 hrs. If your flight lands late, dinner is provided packed.

Day 2

Coyoacán on foot, then a trajinera through Xochimilco

The morning goes to Coyoacán, a neighbourhood of cobbled streets, painted houses, galleries and craft markets. Sit in one of the plazas, look at the colonial churches, and buy something from the stalls. In the afternoon you head for Xochimilco, a UNESCO World Heritage Site better known as the floating gardens of Mexico City. You board a trajinera, a flat boat painted in blocks of colour, and go out along the canals with musicians playing alongside and a Mexican lunch served on board. Afterwards there is time for the streets, churches and busy market of Xochimilco itself.

Overnight in Mexico city. (B, LL & D)

Day 3

The pyramids at Teotihuacán, a guided tour of Mexico City, and

an evening folklore show at Mariachis Square

You drive out to San Juan Teotihuacan, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and walk the Avenue of the Dead between the Pyramid of the Sun and the Pyramid of the Moon. The Palace of Quetzalpapalotl and the Temple of the Feathered Serpent stand on the same site. Afterwards you stop at an obsidian workshop to see how the stone is cut, and to learn about the maguey and agave plants.

Back in Mexico City, the guided tour starts at the Plaza de las Tres Culturas, where Aztec ruins, a Spanish church and modern blocks stand in one square, on the site of the old Aztec city of Tlatelolco. You then see Reforma Avenue, the Metropolitan Cathedral, the Torre Latino and the Palacio Postal, and finish at the Shrine of Our Lady of Guadalupe, one of the most visited holy sites in the world.

The last stop is the Zocalo, the main square of Mexico City and one of the largest public squares anywhere.

In the evening there is a Mexican folklore show at Mariachis Square.

Overnight in Mexico. (B, D)

Day 4

Fly to Merida and tour the city

You check out and fly to Merida, the largest colonial city on the Yucatan Peninsula. A guided tour takes in the Zocalo, the cathedral dedicated to San Ildefonso, the Casa de Montejo, the Government Palace, the Plaza Mayor, the Museum of Contemporary Art, and the Paseo de Montejo, the wide avenue lined with old mansions.

Overnight in Merida. (B, D)

Day 5

Chichen Itza, one of the New 7 Wonders of the World, a cenote swim, and an orientation tour of Valladolid.

You set out for Chichen Itzá, the Maya city that has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1988 and is one of the New 7 Wonders of the World. Before the Spanish arrived, tens of thousands of people lived here. What stands today includes the Pyramid of Kukulkan, the Temple of a Thousand Columns and the great ball court. Next comes a cenote, one of the limestone sinkholes that fill with cool groundwater across the Yucatan, and there is a chance to swim in it. Lunch is a Mexican buffet. Later you drive to Valladolid for an orientation tour: the San Gervasio church, the main square and the painted houses of what is the most important colonial city in Yucatan province.

Overnight in Valladolid. (B, LL & LD)

Note: bring swimwear, a towel, sunscreen and sunglasses

Day 6

A boat safari through the Rio Lagartos reserve, the pink lakes of Las Coloradas, and a chance to try a Mayan mud bath

You drive to Rio Lagartos, a fishing village inside a biosphere reserve recognised by UNESCO for its birds and plant life. A fast boat takes you out through the mangroves to look for flamingos, seabirds and crocodiles, with a guide explaining how the system holds together. Then on to Las Coloradas, where salt ponds turn the water bright pink. There is a chance to try a Mayan mud bath and to swim off the empty beaches on the Gulf of Mexico.

From there the drive carries on to Cancun on the Mexican Caribbean, the best known beach resort in the country. You check into the all-inclusive hotel on arrival.

Overnight in Cancun. (PB, D)

Note: bring swimwear, a towel, sunscreen and sunglasses.

Day 7

A full day at Isla Mujeres, reached by catamaran.

A catamaran takes you across the turquoise water to Isla Mujeres, with an open bar on board. You stop to snorkel over the reef, which is busy with fish, then land on the island for a buffet meal and a bar set up on the beach. The centre of the island is a few streets of craft shops, souvenirs and tequila. The boat brings you back to Cancun in the afternoon.

Overnight in Cancun.(B, D)

Note: bring swimwear, a towel, sunscreen and sunglasses

Day 8

A free day in Cancun, or a trip out to Cozumel

The day is yours. Stay on the beach in Cancun and use the hotel, or take the trip out to Cozumel. That option transfers you to the dock at Playa del Carmen for the ferry, arriving on the island at 9 am, then takes in the Dzul-Ha cenote, Villa Blanca and Paradise Reef, with time in the water.

Overnight in Cancun. (Boxed B, D)

Day 9

Fly home.

You transfer to the airport and the tour ends. If you can spare a few minutes, write and tell us how it went. (B)
