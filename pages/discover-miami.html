        Miami - The City Where the Americas Meet

        Miami sits on the southeast coast of Florida, at the point where the United States and Latin America run into one another. Spanish is as common on the street as English. The beach is fifteen minutes from downtown across a causeway, and the two halves feel like separate cities. Art Deco hotels line Ocean Drive. Murals cover Wynwood. Little Havana smells of coffee and cigars from the middle of the afternoon. It is hot and humid for most of the year, and that shapes how people live here.

        Where to Stay

        • Fontainebleau Miami Beach: Morris Lapidus designed this in 1954, and the curved facade and the staircase in the lobby are still the reason to walk in. It is a large oceanfront resort with several restaurants and a pool scene that runs all afternoon.
        • The Ritz-Carlton, South Beach: A 1953 Art Deco building at the beach end of Lincoln Road, restored rather than rebuilt. Ocean-view rooms, a pool deck facing the water, and Ocean Drive a few blocks south.
        • Mandarin Oriental, Miami: On Brickell Key, a small island off the downtown financial district. Quiet, with a private beach on the bay and rooms looking back at the skyline. The right choice if you want the city without the noise.

        Things to Do

        • Walk South Beach: The Art Deco district runs along Ocean Drive and Collins Avenue, hundreds of buildings in pastel with neon along the fronts at night. Walk it in the early evening once the heat drops, then get on the sand.
        • Wynwood: A warehouse district north of downtown where the walls get painted and repainted by artists from all over. Wynwood Walls is the ticketed courtyard at the centre of it. The free version is every street around it.
        • Little Havana: Calle Ocho is the spine of it. Buy a cortadito at a walk-up window, watch the games at Domino Park, and stop in at one of the shops where cigars are still rolled by hand.

        Eating Out with Family

        Miami feeds a big table well, and most places will find room for one more chair. A few that work with children:

        • Big Pink:
          Address: 157 Collins Ave, Miami Beach, FL 33139
          Big Pink is a diner on South Beach that serves plates meant for sharing whether you planned to share or not. Burgers, breakfast at odd hours, and some of it arriving on a TV dinner tray.
        • Shake Shack:
          Address: Various locations including 1111 Lincoln Rd, Miami Beach, FL 33139
          Shake Shack does burgers, hot dogs and frozen custard shakes quickly and without ceremony. Order at the counter, sit outside, and nobody minds the noise.
        • Bubba Gump Shrimp Co.:
          Address: 401 Biscayne Blvd, Miami, FL 33132
          Bubba Gump sits on the water at Bayside Marketplace and leans hard on the Forrest Gump theme, which children find funnier than adults do. Shrimp every way it comes, plus Southern plates.
        • Cheesecake Factory:
          Address: 19501 Biscayne Blvd, Aventura, FL 33180
          The Cheesecake Factory menu runs to several pages, so everyone finds something and nobody decides quickly. The portions are large, and the cheesecake is the reason to leave room.
        • Graziano's:
          Address: Various locations including 394 Giralda Ave, Coral Gables, FL 33134
          Graziano's is Argentine: grilled meat, empanadas and a wine list longer than it needs to be. It began as a market and still sells cuts over the counter to take home.
        • Whisk Gourmet Food & Catering:
          Address: 7382 SW 56th Ave, South Miami, FL 33143
          Whisk is a small South Miami kitchen doing Southern comfort food from local ingredients. Breakfast, brunch and lunch, and the fried chicken is the thing to order.
        • Los Ranchos Steakhouse:
          Address: 8888 SW 136th St, Miami, FL 33176
          Los Ranchos grills steak Nicaraguan style, with chimichurri and fried plantain alongside. Warm room, big cuts, and enough seafood on the menu to keep a split table happy.

        A Few Things Worth Knowing

        • A crossroads, not a blend: Miami is where North and South America do business, and the city keeps its communities distinct rather than melting them together. You hear it in the food, and in which Spanish is being spoken on which street.
        • Art Deco by the block: South Beach holds the largest concentration of Art Deco buildings anywhere, more than 800 of them, most put up between the 1920s and the early 1940s. A preservation campaign begun in the 1970s is the reason they are still standing.
        • Two seasons, not four: The wet season runs May to October, hot and humid with a storm most afternoons. The dry season, November to April, is why people come in winter. Hurricane season runs June to November.
