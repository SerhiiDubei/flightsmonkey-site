        A Few Days in Montreal

        Montreal sits on an island in the Saint Lawrence River, and it wears its age well. Stone buildings put up in the 1700s stand a few blocks from studios and galleries. The city runs in French, works in both languages, and eats better than it needs to. Come hungry and wear shoes you can walk in.

        Where to Stay

        • Fairmont The Queen Elizabeth: John Lennon and Yoko Ono spent a week in room 1742 here in 1969 and recorded Give Peace a Chance in it. You can still book that room. The rest is a large, well-kept hotel sitting directly on top of the train station.
        • Hotel Le St-James: A former bank on Saint-Jacques Street in Old Montreal, kept ornate on purpose. Sixty rooms, no two laid out the same, and staff who remember what you asked for yesterday.
        • W Montreal: Dark modern rooms in the old Bank of Canada building on Victoria Square, a short walk from Old Montreal. The bar downstairs gets loud at night, which is either the point or the problem.

        Things to Do

        • Walking Old Montreal: The streets here are cobbled and narrow and the buildings go back to the French regime. Start at Place Jacques-Cartier, go inside Notre-Dame Basilica for the blue ceiling, then let the side streets take you wherever they go.
        • Mount Royal Park: Frederick Law Olmsted, who laid out Central Park, designed this one as well. Climb to the Kondiaronk lookout for the view over downtown, or take the flat path round to Beaver Lake and sit for an hour.
        • Montreal Museum of Fine Arts: Five pavilions on Sherbrooke Street, linked underground, holding Old Masters, Quebec painters and contemporary work. The permanent collection on its own takes a couple of hours.

        Where to Eat

        • Joe Beef: A small room on Notre-Dame Street West in Little Burgundy. The menu is chalked on a board and changes with whatever came in that morning. Rich, French-leaning cooking, in portions that assume you skipped lunch.
        • Schwartz's Deli: Open on Saint-Laurent since 1928, selling smoked meat by the sandwich. There is usually a queue, the tables are shared, and the answer is medium-fat unless you already know better.
        • Toqué: Normand Laprise built this place around Quebec growers and fishermen, and the tasting menu moves with the season. It is the expensive night out, and it earns it.

        A Few Things Worth Knowing

        • Two languages, every day: Montreal is the largest French-speaking city in North America and also one of the most bilingual anywhere. Shops and restaurants open in French and switch if you need them to.
        • Festival season: The city runs well over a hundred festivals a year. The International Jazz Festival closes streets downtown in late June, and Just for Laughs follows in July.
        • The underground city: RESO, or la ville souterraine, links metro stations, offices and shopping centres through more than thirty kilometres of tunnel. In January that matters a great deal.
        • Cirque du Soleil started here: It grew out of a troupe of street performers from Baie-Saint-Paul and staged its first full show in Quebec in 1984. The company has been run out of Montreal ever since.
